# TIMBER

## 1. Timber
Timber is wood obtained from trees that is suitable for building, carpentry, and other engineering purposes. It is processed into usable sizes after felling and seasoning.

## 2. Uses of Timber in Buildings
- Doors and windows
- Roof trusses and rafters
- Flooring and ceilings
- Staircases and handrails
- Formwork (shuttering) for concrete
- Scaffolding
- Partitions and panelling
- Furniture and cabinets
- Piles, poles and bridge works

---

## Merits and Demerits of Timber

| **Merits** | **Demerits** |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| High strength-to-weight ratio | Prone to decay due to fungi and insects |
| Easy to cut, shape, and join | Combustible (can catch fire) |
| Good thermal insulation | Absorbs moisture, causing shrinkage and swelling |
| Good sound insulation | Can warp, crack, and twist |
| Attractive natural appearance | Requires regular maintenance |
| Renewable and eco-friendly material | Limited durability in damp conditions |
| Good shock and vibration resistance | Costly for high-quality hardwoods |
| Lightweight, reducing dead load | Susceptible to termite attack if untreated |
| Easily available and repairable | Needs proper seasoning before use |

## 6. Factors Affecting the Use of Timber in Buildings
- Strength and load-bearing capacity
- Durability against weather and decay
- Moisture content and proper seasoning
- Resistance to fungi, termites and insects
- Fire resistance
- Density and weight
- Grain pattern and presence of defects
- Ease of machining and workability
- Availability and cost
- Type of exposure (interior or exterior)
- Maintenance requirements
- Dimensional stability (resistance to shrinkage and swelling)
